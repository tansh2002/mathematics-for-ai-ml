## Why Drop the Traditional Multiplication Sign ($\times$)?

In basic arithmetic, multiplication is written using the cross symbol: $2 \times 3$ [[00:46](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D46)].

However, in algebra, the letter **$x$** is the most commonly used variable [[00:20](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D20)]. Writing "$2 \text{ times } x$" using the traditional symbol looks like:


$$2 \times x$$


This creates ambiguity and confusion—it can easily be misread as "two $x$ $x$" or two operations next to each other [[01:08](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D68)]. To avoid this, algebra drops the $\times$ symbol [[01:28](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D88)].

## The 3 Ways to Write Multiplication in Algebra

Instead of $\times$, algebra uses three standard notations [[01:45](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D105)]:

| Notation | Example | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dot Notation** | $2 \cdot x$ | A centered dot placed slightly higher than a decimal point [[01:45](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D105)]. |
| **Parentheses** | $2(x)$ | Placing the variable or number inside parentheses directly next to the factor [[02:06](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D126)]. |
| **Juxtaposition (Side-by-side)** | $2x$ | Writing the number directly next to the variable (most common) [[02:22](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D142)]. |

> **Why couldn't we do this with numbers alone?**
> Writing two numbers side-by-side like $2\ 3$ looks like twenty-three ($23$), not $2 \times 3$ [[02:39](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D159)]. But when combining a number and a letter ($2x$), it is immediately clear that it means "2 times $x$" [[02:57](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D177)].

### Key Takeaway for Notes

* **$2x$**, **$2 \cdot x$**, and **$2(x)$** all mean the exact same thing: **$2 \text{ multiplied by } x$** [[03:02](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D182)].
* Always follow the **Order of Operations (PEMDAS)**: evaluate the multiplication before addition or subtraction [[03:41](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DvDaIKB19TvY%26t%3D221)].
